# Panoramic sensor performance diagnosis

During the guided collection stage, the CUDA brain was active but whole-GPU utilization remained low. Each world also computes 1,928 ray intersections against 112 boxes in NumPy, advances physics and records data. The isolated sensor benchmark identifies substantial CPU cost; it does not imply the connectome is unused.

A private prototype computes those same intersections across eight worlds with float64 CUDA arithmetic, then produces the existing float32 observations. In one repeated static benchmark under concurrent training, sensor calculation took approximately 0.324 seconds per NumPy batch and 0.00795 seconds per CUDA batch. Observations for the eight original poses were bitwise equal. This is about 40.7 times faster for sensor calculation alone, not an end-to-end training speedup.

A further check compared 128 reconstructed optimization poses spanning the collected guided trace. Maximum sensor difference was below 0.000000000000001; eleven of sixteen batches were bitwise equal. These were static observations of existing training data, without physical transitions, evaluation or optimizer updates.

The prototype was then compared with the original environment using two complete connectome instances, each with 167,184 neurons and 25,583,622 connections. Eight steps across eight worlds in each backend made 128 verification transitions in total. Physical states and rewards were equal. Maximum neural-history difference was about 0.00000021, with a small difference already present between the independently computed initial GPU neural readouts. Measured environment-step time was approximately eight times faster in that short check. It is not a prediction of sustained training throughput or navigation quality.

The active panoramic-neural-v7 experiment retains its frozen NumPy runtime. The acceleration is isolated in private and has not replaced its sources, changed its budget or modified any checkpoint. A second 128-transition verification included a forced collision, terminal observations and a fresh episode; both backends agreed within the same neural tolerance, with identical physical states and rewards. Its measured step-time ratio was about 5.5 rather than eight, which illustrates the limits of a short timing measurement. Production integration and broader mode/shape compatibility remain pending. Guided arrivals remain teacher outcomes; autonomous student performance must be measured separately.
